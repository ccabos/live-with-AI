Capability. Value. Worth.

Three things rode together for all of human history. Cheap machine cognition is pulling them apart — and every question the AI age asks is a question about the seams.

01 The sentence § I

Where the argument starts

“AI is becoming more intelligent than us” hides three claims

Nearly every muddled argument about our future comes from sliding between them — and almost all the public noise is conducted in the key of the one we can't yet answer.

Claim 01 — narrow

Machines beat us at particular tasks: chess, protein folding, reading a scan. True, old news, and long since made peace with. No one feels diminished by a calculator.

Claim 02 — the one that matters

Machines match or beat the typical person across most of the cognitive work our economies and institutions actually run on. Not one game — the general capacity to read, reason, write, plan, explain. This is arriving now.

Claim 03 — speculative

A superintelligence that outstrips all of us at everything, including improving itself. May come; reasonable people disagree. The films are made of this one — and it crowds out the claim that is already true.

02 The thesis § II–III

The central move

“Intelligence” is not one thing — and the machine is colonizing the list from the top

The flat sentence treats a tall stack of distinct capacities as a single height to be measured. Walk the stack and the shape of everything that follows is already visible: the capacities at the bottom are precisely the ones the future economy and our common life will have to be rebuilt around.

Knowledge recall and synthesis of what is already known

machine: superhuman

Fluid reasoning working out the genuinely new, in the moment

machine: rising fast, uneven

Embodied a body in an unstructured world — the plumber's hands, the cook's improvisation

machine: clumsy, expensive

Social & relational reading a room, building trust, being with another person

machine: mimics the surface

Moral intelligence & wisdom weighing what ought to matter, standing behind the consequences

machine: not in the room

The bottom rungs may not be automatable at all — they require a subject who has something at stake, who can be held to account, who can suffer the consequences of being wrong. A tool cannot be the responsible party.

03 The seams § IV–VI

Where the unbundling lands

Three places the ground shifts

Nothing collapses dramatically. Load-bearing assumptions — that human cognition is scarce and therefore precious — quietly stop being true, and arrangements that felt like human nature turn out to have been contingent on them.

Living together — § IV

  • Authority moves from who knows to who judges and answers — or curdles into populism plus a new priesthood of oracle-owners.
  • Truth stops living in the artifact. When anything can be synthesized, evidence migrates to provenance: whose word stands behind it, and what do they lose if it's false?
  • Education must decide what it is for — loading knowledge into heads is obsolete; forming judgment, taste and attention is more urgent than ever.
  • Agency: every tool atrophies something. The question is which faculties we deliberately keep, not whether we lose any.

The economy — § V

  • Repricing: cognitive output drifts toward the cost of electricity even as its quantity explodes — more valuable work done, less of it captured as any wage.
  • The broken link: productivity stops flowing to pay once human cognition leaves the critical path. Gains flow to what stays scarce — compute, energy, data, trust, distribution.
  • The real fault line is not humans vs machines but owners of the scarce complements vs sellers of cognition. Political to the core.
  • Value ≠ money: care, community, a healthy river — always the substance of a good life, always invisible to the ledger. Even the UN now proposes to count what GDP leaves out.

Work — § VI

  • Wrong question: “what can humans still do?” is a race on the machine's track, retreating yearly.
  • What the world will demand: the physical and unstructured; accountability (the one who signs); the interface that directs machines and judges output.
  • What we want to keep: care, making and stewarding, governing ourselves — chosen, not forced.
  • The transition is the story: the weavers never saw the better world their suffering purchased. Whether this is liberation or betrayal turns on sharing the gains and cushioning the fall.

04 The counsel § VII–VIII

What to do about it

Strive for a different intelligence — and watch who holds the machines

We do not lift weights because the world suffers a shortage of heavy things that need moving. The point was never the moved weight; it was the changed body. So with thinking. § VII — whether to strive for intelligence

The race on the machine's axes — recall, calculation — is lost and not worth running. What's worth cultivating is what the machine does not have: wisdom (which things are worth doing), discernment (the true from the merely fluent), the meta-cognitive skill of auditing an intelligence greater than your own without being captured by it, and the intelligence of meaning itself. And this is not a hobby: a population that gratefully stops thinking becomes trivially steerable by whoever owns the machine. Independent thought becomes a civic duty — the inner equivalent of a free press.

CLOSED WEIGHTS

capability exists, but is held
→ rented through a metered interface
→ monitored, priced, revocable at will
→ gains captured; trust taken on faith
→ power concentrates

OPEN WEIGHTS

the thing itself, in anyone's hands
→ run by a hospital, a town, a nation
→ inspectable, auditable, survivable
→ a commons rather than a metered utility
→ power diffuses

The honest cost. A model released cannot be recalled, and openness puts capability into hands that will misuse it. The dilemma is real and unresolved — anyone who tells you it's easy, in either direction, is selling something. What open weights decide is not how high the frontier reaches, but how widely the reach is shared.

05 The map § IX–X

The futures — regions, not predictions

Two variables select among four futures

The trajectory of capability, crossed with the distribution of control. The essay refuses a forecast, because the forecast would repeat the mistake it argues against: the belief that the technology decides.

capability · steady climb → sharp takeoff

Singleton

Sharp takeoff, concentrated control. One power sets the terms for everyone, perhaps permanently — the dream of wise order and the nightmare of irreversible tyranny, distinguished by nothing in the technology and everything in the governance.

Wildfire

Sharp takeoff, wide diffusion. Maximum capability in maximum hands — the fullest freedom and the fullest catastrophic risk at once; the openness dilemma at civilizational scale.

Feudal abundance

Steady climb, concentrated control. Plenty exists, even comfort distributed downward to keep the peace — but rented, not owned, and the say-so pools among the few.

the default path

Pluralist

Steady climb, wide diffusion. Disruptive but broadly empowering, like electricity or the press — messy, contested, and tending toward leveling.

the reachable good
control: concentrated control: diffuse
Mundane — capability simply plateaus: a powerful but bounded tool, transformative the way the PC and the internet were. Badly underrated, drowned out by the takeoff drama, and maybe the likeliest base case of all.
Meaning crisis — can coexist with any of the above, even the materially successful ones: a world that has solved production but not purpose. Abundance is not the same as meaning, and a society can win the first while losing the second.
The case in point — § IX. The software engineer lives in the forecast world already, and builds the systems automating her. Her chapter — including what supervising a fleet of machines actually costs the person doing it — has its own page.

06 The choice § XI

Where the argument lands

At every turn, what mattered was not how intelligent the machines became, but what we chose to do about it.

Whether authority flattens or curdles; whether gains are shared or pooled; whether the displaced are carried or abandoned; whether capability diffuses or concentrates; whether freed energy goes into meaning or drifts in comfortable purposelessness — none of it is decided by the technology. The refusal to choose is itself a choice, usually of the default path. The hardest problems of the age are not technical but human — distribution, power, meaning — and those are exactly the problems no machine can solve for us, because they are about what we want.
